Kitchin

Mobile app · UI/UX & HCI research

Know what you have.
Cook with care. Give with heart.

A smart-fridge companion that tracks expiry dates, suggests recipes from what you already have, and helps you give food to food banks and orphanages before it goes to waste.

Kitchin in motion

Everything in one place

Groceries, the fridge, recipes and charity all start from home.

    01 · The problem

    Good food, quietly wasted.

    Busy households buy food with the best intentions. Then life happens, and a lot of it never gets eaten.

    • 01

      Food expires unnoticed

      Items get pushed to the back of the fridge and forgotten until they have gone off.

    • 02

      "What do I cook with this?"

      Leftover ingredients go unused because it is hard to think of a meal that fits what is left.

    • 03

      Shopping without seeing

      Without knowing what is at home, it is easy to buy things twice or forget what is missing.

    • 04

      Nowhere for spare food to go

      Sealed food a family will not finish often ends up in the bin instead of with people who need it.

    03 · How Kitchin helps

    Three habits, one app.

    Know what's about to expire

    Connect your smart fridge by scanning its QR code. Every item is tracked by name, category and expiry date, and anything new can be added by scanning its barcode or typing it in. Items close to their date are flagged, so nothing gets forgotten.

    • Error preventionSet fields for name, category, expiry date and quantity cut down on mistakes.
    • Recognition over recallScanning, product photos and category filters mean people recognise items instead of remembering them.

    Cook with what you already have

    Recipes are recommended from the ingredients in your fridge. Filter by cuisine and difficulty, then see at a glance which ingredients you have and which are missing, with clear cooking steps to follow.

    • Match with the real worldFamiliar food categories, cuisines and photos make choosing a dish feel natural.

    Give it to someone who needs it

    Tap From Fridge to donate sealed food to the nearest food bank. Tap Browse Items to buy what a food bank or orphanage needs most. At checkout, Round up to donate turns small change into a donation.

    • ConsistencyButtons share the same shapes and colours across the flow.
    • EfficiencyDonating follows one short, simple path.
    • FeedbackThe next screen confirms the chosen items, so people know it worked.

    04 · Testing it

    Putting it in people's hands.

    How I tested

    Participants
    24 Digital Media Technology and Software Engineering students
    Age
    18 to 23
    Study
    Quantitative, using the System Usability Scale (SUS)
    Setup
    Laptop, phone or tablet, consent form, Figma prototype and Google Forms questionnaire
    1. UI design review
    2. Prototype testing
    3. Questionnaire & feedback

    Tasks people completed

    • Connect a smart fridge to the app
    • Buy groceries, from browsing to checkout and delivery tracking
    • Donate expiring food, or round up a bill for charity
    • Edit their profile

    Overall usability score

    85.4/ 100

    Excellent usability with high satisfaction. The usual SUS average is around 68.
